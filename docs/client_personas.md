# Client Personas

## Fundamental Analyst

A fundamental analyst needs complete financial statements to compare company performance. Revenue, income, assets, liabilities, and equity must use consistent metric definitions across companies and years. Source tags, filing dates, and period dates provide traceability when a number needs to be checked. Completeness, consistency, and traceability therefore matter most for this user.

## Quant Researcher

A quant researcher needs timely and consistently formatted market prices for repeated analysis. Missing dates or duplicate records can distort returns, signals, and backtests. Price fields must also follow basic rules, such as high being greater than or equal to low. Timeliness, uniqueness, completeness, and validity are the main quality dimensions for this user.

## Credit Analyst

A credit analyst focuses on assets, liabilities, equity, and how leverage changes across periods. These values must be complete and use comparable definitions before ratios are calculated. The balance-sheet equation and period-to-period checks help identify inconsistent records that require investigation. Completeness and consistency are especially important for credit analysis.

## Fit for Purpose

Fit for purpose means that the same dataset may be suitable for one client use case but unsuitable for another. Annual financial data may support fundamental analysis, for example, but it is not timely enough for intraday trading decisions.
